# Narrative continuity and usable communication style

Read this reference when composing a profile, improving its reading experience, or extracting communication style. Preserve all useful supported detail. Change how the information connects, not how much valuable information survives.

## Open with a substantial portrait of the person now

The overview should let a new reader understand who the subject is at this stage of life, what occupies their time and attention, and what makes their situation distinctive. Write it after extracting and organizing the supported understanding, but display it first. It is a developed introduction, not a navigation blurb, compressed resume, personality verdict, or miniature list of the five modules.

Select the details that make the present understandable:

- **Current work or main activity:** establish the person's actual role and setting, what they are doing or taking responsibility for, and any important parallel commitment. For company-based work, clarify company, product or service, and contribution without opening with a catalogue of organizations or technical features. For other life contexts, start with the activities that actually matter.
- **Current life:** include supported non-work circumstances, meaningful routines, learning, interests, community activity, or personal projects when they help explain everyday life now. Retain distinctive relevant details instead of relegating all non-work material to a generic hobbies sentence. Do not invent work-life balance, location, relationships, or lifestyle from a lack of evidence.
- **Present attention and concerns:** explain the goals, choices, practical questions, recurring interests, or commitments that the subject is actively engaging with. Ground them in explicit statements or substantive activity. Exposure to a topic, a newsletter, or a single purchase does not automatically establish a priority. A concern need not be worry; do not infer anxiety, frustration, ambition, or hidden motivation from circumstance alone.
- **Selective earlier context:** use a small amount of relevant prior experience to explain the present, keeping current circumstances central. Do not retell the full educational or employment history, lead with old credentials by default, or manufacture a causal career narrative.

These are lenses for selection, not mandatory subsections or a work-life-concerns paragraph formula. Their order and relative space follow the subject and the material. Work, life, and interests may connect naturally or remain distinct; do not invent a link to make a smoother story. Present circumstances deserve most of the attention, but no numerical ratio or fixed length is required. Use enough connected paragraphs to make the portrait independently readable without copying the full detailed modules.

Do not squeeze an extensive profile into a few short sentences. Also do not expand a sparse source with generic biography. If authorized sources reveal mainly work, write the supported work-centered portrait and note that coverage limit once in the source overview when useful, rather than filling the opening with missing-data statements. Uncertainty that changes the central interpretation still belongs next to the relevant claim; do not turn offers into employment or tentative plans into completed transitions for narrative convenience.

Communication or working habits may appear where they help readers recognize the person, but they do not have to receive a sentence each in the overview. Let actions, interests, responsibilities, and meaningful choices reveal character; do not replace this with a string of labels such as driven, thoughtful, or efficient. Keep current priorities scoped to the present instead of turning them into permanent traits.

Check the overview on its own: can the reader describe the person's present situation, what they are doing, and what currently matters, using concrete details? If the reader learns only a job title, a list of companies, and broad style adjectives, the opening has not done its job. Every factual statement remains supported by active derived observations. During incremental updates, revise the overview only when new information changes this current picture.

## Natural prose throughout the profile

Write as an attentive person introducing someone whose actual circumstances they understand, in idiomatic English, the published language. Specificity, proportion, and fluent connections create a human voice. Do not simulate intimacy, imitate the subject's writing voice, add slang or jokes by default, or fictionalize private scenes and feelings.

Keep the stable top-level modules as navigation. Inside them, choose the number of paragraphs, descriptive subheadings, sentence lengths, and occasional lists to suit the actual content. A paragraph may explain a relationship, follow a decision through its consequences, or describe an everyday practice. It does not need the same claim-evidence-implication pattern as the next paragraph. The extraction dimensions are prompts for discovery, not a prose template or mandatory set of subheadings.

Establish the name naturally, then vary sentence construction or omit repeated subjects where the language permits. Prefer ordinary verbs and precise nouns to abstract managerial language. Use transitions when relationships need explaining, not automatic connectors at every paragraph. Let contextual differences sit naturally together rather than forcing a dramatic contrast or a tidy concluding moral.

Avoid repeated stock openings, praise that would fit almost anyone, an equal-sized paragraph for every facet, and repeated evidence disclaimers after each detail. Keep material uncertainty visible, but summarize routine provenance once at source level. Natural writing does not weaken factual distinctions, remove useful names and specifics, or turn a profile into an embellished biography. It also does not require casualness: an understated factual account can still feel personal and readable.

Review the draft for patterns that came from the template rather than the person: repeated sentence shapes, the same length for every passage, boilerplate transitions, and sections that merely restate their headings. Adjust those passages for meaning and flow; do not introduce random variation merely to sound less uniform. A useful final test is whether replacing the subject's name would leave the account equally applicable to almost anyone. If so, look for supported circumstances and choices that the prose flattened.

## Compose a document, not an observation dump

Use the resolved display name or reliably extracted name as the narrative subject, following SKILL.md. Write about that person in third person throughout Background's opening and the substantive account. Use the name where needed with natural connections or subject omission; avoid second-person address and repetitive name insertion.

Keep the five top-level modules in the established order. Inside a rich module, use descriptive topical headings and connected paragraphs as useful. Each paragraph should develop a coherent idea; context, relationships, and distinctions belong where they help the reader, without a prescribed sentence order. Related observations can remain separate in state while appearing together in one passage.

Within Background's professional account, make each company the organizing unit: current primary work first, other active concurrent work next, then concise earlier roles. Keep the role, products, people, customers, and progress of one company together. Integrate meaningful current life context alongside the current account according to its relevance, rather than automatically placing it after the entire career history. A short public company introduction can orient the reader, followed by the subject's actual contribution grounded in personal sources. See [Company-centered current work](company-context.md).

Make continuity explicit. Explain how products relate to the user's responsibilities, how a collaborator fits into a project, why a communication choice differs by audience, or how a current role follows earlier work when evidence supports that relationship. Use specific nouns and natural transitions; do not invent causal explanations merely to make prose flow.

Avoid one heading or bullet per fact, repetitive sentence openings, generic trait labels, disconnected addenda, and a single wall of text. Removing bullet symbols alone does not solve fragmented writing. Use a numbered list when the content is an actual sequence and a short variant list when readers need to compare situations. Rich information can justify substantial paragraphs and multiple subsections without a fixed length or count.

Background's opening paragraph introduces the person, while the modules preserve detailed understanding. A compact fact table may aid navigation but cannot replace either account. Do not copy a reference layout's decorations or force a sidebar when prose is sufficient. During work, give connected progress updates about the overall result rather than streaming every newly found claim. Keep the final profile self-contained.

The published document follows [TeamHub publication](teamhub-publication.md). Its prose must cover every retained observation and preserve its meaning. During updates, revise every related passage together so old claims do not remain in prose after correction or removal.

## Turn observations into an explanation

Keep discovery, synthesis, and delivery distinct. Individual observations support updates; they do not prescribe the visible outline. Before drafting, form a transient account of the supported relationships and of the few explanations that organize them. Then write the module as a whole. Associate paragraphs with their supporting blocks afterward rather than allowing block order to dictate paragraph order.

For a company-based professional account in Background, establish the subject's main current company or team, its relevant product, and the person's actual role without making the reader assemble clues from several sections. Continue inside that company: explain the product's purpose, what the subject changes or owns, how collaborators and customers fit that work, and what meaningful progress is established. Preserve other current commitments and life context before concise relevant history. Products are part of their company's account; do not make product, people, and milestones into disconnected peer sections. Keep legal-employer uncertainty local to the relevant statement without allowing it to erase an established team/product relationship. This company organization does not require every opening overview to start with employment when another supported circumstance is more central to the person's present life.

In behavioral modules, develop a supported interpretation through concrete behavior and its practical consequence. For instance, an observed habit of stating completion status and asking what remains can support a scoped insight about making the next action clear. It does not establish an unobserved motive, personality diagnosis, or universal preference for short messages. Distinguish what the person explicitly values from what a repeated behavior appears to accomplish.

Preserve differences that explain the person. The same subject may communicate differently across relationships, purposes, genres, or levels of familiarity. Explain a supported difference in continuous prose instead of creating an independent mini-profile for every category. Do not assume which setting receives more detail or which tone the subject uses. Use a subheading when the subject changes meaningfully, such as another company; do not add one merely because another observation is available.

The final account must still contain the useful concrete detail. A company name, actual deliverable, named comparison, supported customer group, handoff, meaningful result, or contextual exception should not disappear into broad claims about strategy, efficiency, ownership, or clarity. Conversely, adding a list of specific nouns without explaining their relationships is not synthesis.

## Communication style: extract voice, emphasis, and habits

The central questions are what this person sounds like, what their messages foreground, and how those qualities appear in expression. A record of replying to administration, assigning research, or asking for a usable deliverable describes activity. It becomes a style insight only when the account identifies the tone, interpersonal stance, and observable expression choices. Drafting guidance may follow that portrait; it must not replace it.

Inspect the following dimensions wherever the material supports them. They are universal discovery questions, not required conclusions, a fixed number of traits, or a prescribed message formula.

| Dimension | What to identify | What makes the insight concrete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tone and register | Degree of formality, conversational ease, warmth, restraint, enthusiasm, confidence, caution, and directness | Connect the description to observable choices: personal acknowledgment, reserved or expressive wording, hedging or definite claims, questions or directives, explanation before disagreement, and how courtesy accompanies a request. Warmth, confidence, and pressure are separate dimensions; do not bundle them into a stereotype. |
| Emphasis and priorities | What receives attention or is repeatedly placed before the main request | Identify what carries the argument: recipient relevance, proof, practical benefit, relationship, empathy, precision, urgency, choice, or another observed concern. Explain what this looks like in the person's messages. Do not infer an inner motive, label, or universal value from a single choice. |
| Opening | How the recipient is addressed and what the first substantive move does | Describe the greeting form and whether the opening establishes familiarity, acknowledges the recipient, supplies context, asks a question, or states a conclusion. A personalized reference and a generic courtesy serve different functions. Preserve that difference without copying either sentence. |
| Progression | How a message advances from opening to purpose and response | Recover the actual order of moves and important shorter or longer variants. Explain how one move prepares the next. Do not impose a universal number of stages or assume every communication is persuasion. |
| Request and response sought | What action is requested, how explicitly, and with what commitment or pressure | Distinguish a decision from feedback, an invitation from an instruction, a definite deadline from an optional next step, and an open choice from a constrained one. Describe whether permission, alternatives, reasons, urgency, or room to decline accompany the request. Keep material variants and their contexts rather than calling every polite close a soft ask. |
| Pace and format | How the person packages information in the observed genre | Describe brevity or elaboration, paragraph rhythm, scanning aids, and any supported use or non-use of lists. Numerical ranges require inspected samples and must be scoped to the genre; they are not requirements for the profile's own length. |
| Closing and identity | How the interpersonal exchange ends and how the sender is represented | Distinguish the final request from the sign-off; describe closing formality and whether a given name, full name, role, or team identity is used. Explain supported audience differences without reproducing signatures, personal identifiers, or persona names. An automatic footer is a separate source feature. |
| Contextual variation | What changes with familiarity, audience, purpose, language, or channel | State the relevant contrast and what stays stable. Do not reduce this to different audiences need different styles; name the actual difference in tone, opening, detail, request, or closing. Do not assume internal messages are always brief or external ones always warm. |
| Limits and exceptions | A supported avoidance, adaptation, or boundary of the observed pattern | Scope it precisely. A format missing from a small sample does not prove a dislike; an observed habit does not justify always or never. Current message status and older signatures do not establish personality. |

## Attribute the communication approach correctly

Read the relevant exchange broadly before distinguishing what each part supports. Authorship is a claim-specific check, not a source-selection filter. Use attributable prose and edits for the person's own wording. Incoming replies, specific feedback, and reports of interactions may help explain the subject's communication choices, priorities, and reception, with appropriate scope; they can also document substantive actions and preferences. Do not transfer the sender's tone to the subject or treat a recipient's interpretation as a definitive account of intent. A quotation, coworker's draft, shared-inbox message, or automatically generated message cannot be silently credited as the subject's own writing.

Keep two potentially useful conclusions distinct:

- **Personally authored voice:** what attributable writing or edits show about how the person naturally expresses a message in that setting.
- **A communication approach the person uses:** a structure, tone, or request strategy that the person demonstrably selects, approves, adapts, or deploys for a particular kind of work. AI assistance or a template does not automatically make this adopted approach irrelevant. State it as the approach used in that context, not proof that the person personally wrote every phrase or speaks that way everywhere.

There must be evidence connecting the subject to the choice or use. A message arriving in the mailbox, a shared sender address, or mere possession of a draft is insufficient. If that connection is unresolved, retain useful campaign or work context without attributing a personal style. Do not automatically assume AI authorship just because a message is polished or repetitive.

Many copies of one template establish neither independent examples of natural voice nor a cross-context habit. A demonstrably used template can still establish that campaign's pattern; broaden beyond it before describing the person's general communication. Authored body text and automatic signature content may support different conclusions within the same message.

## Compare relevant contexts before synthesizing

Inspect representative communication across the purposes and relationships that actually matter to the subject. Do not let whichever messages are easiest to summarize become the whole style portrait. A large campaign should not drown out important personal correspondence, and a few administrative confirmations should not stand in for substantive work communication. There is no fixed quota; breadth follows the available material and source scope.

Transiently compare the context, attributable expression choices, apparent tone, repeated emphasis, message progression, response sought, and meaningful exceptions. Preserve a pattern only at the level the evidence supports. Clear self-statements may establish preferences directly; describe isolated expression choices narrowly. Do not keep raw samples or a per-message comparison file.

Current conversation can add observed feedback style when in scope, but it does not substitute for the requested communication corpus. An instruction about what this Skill should produce is a task requirement, not a personal trait. Repeated attributable behavior in a chat can support a context-specific observation; do not make dissatisfaction with a draft the organizing explanation of the person's entire communication style.

If available evidence supports only one genre, name that genre and describe its actual voice and habits. If coverage is thin, make targeted reads for promising gaps or state the limit once. Do not fill gaps with general advice or weaken every supported sentence with evidence-reporting boilerplate.

## Compose a recognizable style portrait

Begin with a coherent account of tone and emphasis: the supported quality, the expression choices producing it, and its scope. Then connect the opening, message progression, request, and closing as habits that express that approach. Explain meaningful audience or purpose variants at the point where they change the interpretation. Keep the observable habits in the profile itself, not only in internal blocks.

Useful topical subheadings may separate tone and emphasis, structure and habits, or contextual variants when the material warrants them. These are reading aids, not mandatory headings. Coherence does not require removing all subheadings or forcing everything into a wall of prose. A sequence can clarify an actually observed progression; a short list can compare genuinely different request variants. Surround them with the explanation that relates them to the person's approach.

Describe supported style directly. Do not turn the section into a chronology of messages, a list of audiences, an account of completed tasks, an extended record of feedback about this Skill, or a tutorial dominated by should and needs to. At the end, a concise practical implication can help a future draft preserve the observed voice.

Every important style claim should pass this chain: **supported tone or emphasis -> observable expression choices -> relevant context or variation**. For instance, confidence is not a synonym for aggressive pressure, warmth is not a synonym for informality, and shortness is not a synonym for vagueness. Explain the combination actually observed without assigning desirable adjectives by default.

### Independently synthetic specificity example

The following editorial setting is invented to demonstrate specificity, not inferred from a real user's examples. None of its habits are defaults for future profiles.

Weak: Rowan communicates professionally and values clarity.

More specific: In manuscript reviews, Rowan's tone is restrained and analytical. Comments identify the claim at issue and the reasoning behind a requested change before discussing optional wording. Corrections needed for factual accuracy are stated directly, while matters of style are offered as alternatives. This makes the priority visible: the strength of the argument receives more attention than surface polish. A reply should address the substantive concern first, while leaving room to discuss the suggested wording.

The stronger account describes tone, emphasis, structure, and differing levels of firmness through recognizable behavior. Its final drafting implication is supported by the portrait; it does not replace it. A real profile requires evidence for those choices and should keep any additional useful habits or exceptions the material supports.

## Divide the modules without splitting the explanation

**Communication Style** owns the interpersonal strategy: the audience, purpose, tone, opening-to-closing sequence, response sought, and contextual variants. Include enough structure, greeting, closing, and length detail to make that account usable.

**Writing Style** owns expression mechanics: sentence rhythm, paragraph construction, vocabulary, punctuation, formatting, linguistic register, and genre differences. Add distinct detail here instead of repeating the whole communication section. A short natural reference to another module is acceptable; the communication account must still stand on its own.

**Working Style** explains how responsibilities are carried out: the supported process, decisions, rhythm, collaboration, assistance, follow-through, and contextual variation. Read [Concrete working practices](working-style.md) for extraction and composition. Keep a review handoff or tool's operational function here, while keeping the tone and framing of a review request in Communication Style.

**Preferences** contains the user's stated or well-supported preferences for receiving information and collaborating. What someone usually writes is not automatically what they want to receive, nor an instruction for every future assistant response.

## Useful phrasing without retaining raw messages

Keep derived descriptions, functional formulas, and abstract templates in the output language. Do not copy source sentences, exact email closings with private identity/contact details, complete signature blocks, message subjects, quotations, or translations of excerpts.

A functional template can specify a greeting addressed to the recipient by name, followed by a relevant observation and a low-commitment next step, without retaining the original wording. If an illustrative sentence would materially improve usability, write a new generic example with placeholders and clearly label it as newly composed. It illustrates the supported pattern; it is not a quotation, a past event, or additional evidence. Do not reconstruct a real message by changing a few names.

The screenshot or another profile is a reference for useful dimensions and reading quality, not verified evidence about the person. Do not transplant its exact sequence, sentence-count range, greeting rule, sign-off names, personas, company history, or reported issue into a new profile without support.

Treat an automatic-signature mismatch separately from writing style. Preserve a concise derived issue only when supported, with the scope and uncertainty of the observation. Do not repeat contact details or treat the old signature as current employment. A profile task does not authorize changing signatures or sending messages.

## Independently synthetic composition example

The following names and events are invented for instruction and do not describe an actual user. This example shows how relationships and observed behavior support a connected account; its occupation, activities, and tone must never become extraction defaults.

Mira coordinates community events at Juniper Books, an independent bookseller. Its school reading programme combines author visits with small-group workshops. Mira arranges the school sessions and prepares the activities, while a colleague manages book orders. The current expansion adds sessions at a second venue, so venue coordination now sits alongside the existing school relationships. That change broadens the programme's delivery needs; it does not establish that Mira manages the whole business.

In workshop planning, Mira uses questions and alternative activities to keep teachers involved in shaping a session. Requests for changes are accompanied by a description of how each option affects the children participating. The resulting tone is exploratory and consultative: a useful response should engage with the options and their implications, rather than treating the first proposal as a settled instruction. Messages confirming a booked event become more definite about arrangements. The distinction is between shaping the session together and coordinating an agreed event, not evidence that Mira always avoids firm decisions.

A real account needs evidence for every corresponding relationship and behavior. This example illustrates explanatory connections, not a required paragraph count, an approved tone, or a template whose names should be replaced.

## Usability and continuity review

Read the section as someone about to draft a new message. Can they choose an appropriate opening, progress through the body, frame the ask, close, and adapt the approach to the supported audience? If the evidence is rich but the answer remains only professional and concise, return to extraction or synthesis.

Review the actual output, not only the instructions or supporting inventory:

- A new reader can explain where the person currently works, which product belongs to that company, and what the person actually does after reading the opening account.
- Behavioral claims contain recognizable actions, contextual distinctions, and a useful implication. Replacing the name with almost anyone else's would change their fit; generic praise fails this check.
- Each paragraph develops the previous context or makes a meaningful transition. If paragraphs can be randomly reordered without affecting comprehension, examine whether the module remains a collection of disconnected facts.
- The text answers what the person sounds like and what they pay attention to, rather than substituting instructions for writing a standard professional email.
- Detail remains in the delivered prose, not only in transient notes. Brief source attribution and necessary uncertainty do not interrupt every sentence.
- The existing authorized profile is actually revised when that is the requested repair. A comparison table, proposed example, or updated Skill alone is not the finished profile.

These are editorial checks, not automatic proof of extraction quality. State source limits briefly, do not invent connective facts, and retain concrete context that makes the account specific to the person.
