# Acceptance scenarios

Use these scenarios when changing extraction, merging, or publication. All examples are fictional. They describe expected behavior, not actual Gmail coverage demonstrated by any test.

## Primary lifecycle checks

- **First extraction without a prior Profile:** a new subject provides one authorized mailbox and no previous profile. Investigate relevant information across authors, periods, and all five modules; deliver concrete working practices, recognizable communication tone, and connected current context where supported. Do not wait for a repair request to discover those dimensions.
- **First profile already useful:** a rich source is available. The first delivery retains distinct valuable detail with a general source overview, rather than delivering a thin placeholder and depending on daily updates to fill it. Actual access limits remain explicit.
- **Daily addition:** one new development affects one current activity. Read the new material and only necessary older context, update the related facts and passages, and preserve other modules. Do not rerun broad historical discovery.
- **No useful changes:** a successful incremental scan adds no meaningful understanding. Preserve profile prose, advance only the successfully completed source boundary, and do not activate the recomposition guard or manufacture a new trait.
- **Interrupted or missing boundary:** an interrupted source keeps its prior boundary; another completed source may advance. A legacy profile without a boundary uses a disclosed bounded recent window to establish one, rather than automatic full re-extraction or treating an edit timestamp as a source read.
- **Explicit exceptions:** a requested section correction affects that section and its dependent claims. A full historical rebuild requires a request for that scope. Updating the Skill or running it daily does not itself authorize either operation or create a schedule.

## Breadth and useful retained detail

1. **Only Gmail is connected.** Received and sent correspondence supports professional context and several behavioral observations. Produce the supported profile without demanding another connector. One source can support many observations.
2. **Rich incoming work context.** Incoming product discussions, assignments, partnership exchanges, and client delivery records establish organizations, products, responsibilities, named collaborators, customer segments, and milestones. Retain all distinct useful supported relationships, not just a generic job label.
3. **No sent-mail access.** Build a substantial account from relevant incoming correspondence and records, including supported responsibilities, actions, relationships, and scoped preferences. Limit only the conclusions affected by missing authored material. Do not make the profile empty, restrict incoming material to background, or infer the subject's wording from other writers.
4. **Mixed automation.** An advertisement and spam are excluded. Relevant HR, release notices, non-promotional industry updates, client notifications, and transaction records are assessed individually. A source category or automatic sender is not itself a rejection rule.
5. **Uneven module depth.** Background has extensive supported detail while writing evidence is sparse. Preserve the rich module without imposing equal lengths or filling the weak module with speculation.
6. **Many useful points.** A rich source supports substantially more observations than a brief profile. Retain every distinct relevant observation with useful context. There is no point range or cap; only true repetition is removed.
7. **Single-insight first pass.** Investigate missing incoming mail, archived material, shallow snippets, narrow topics, and premature merging. If a real limitation still permits only one observation, deliver it and explain the actual limit without fabricating more.
8. **Professional context evolves.** Preserve useful current projects, client work, role changes, and career developments with accurate time/status. Do not discard them for failing to be lifelong traits.

## Attribution and status

9. **Another person's writing.** A collaborator's email may establish the subject's relationship, action, decision, or a specifically reported preference. The sender's wording, tone, requests, and opinions are not automatically the subject's style or preferences. Assess individual claims rather than rejecting the message or treating everything it contains as confirmed.
10. **Assignment versus completion.** A request assigns responsibility. Without completion evidence, describe the assignment rather than the delivered outcome or a stable work habit.
11. **Invitation and silence.** An invitation without a reply proves neither attendance nor a missed meeting. Never infer acceptance, rejection, or disinterest from silence.
12. **Recruiting sequence.** A real offer may be retained as career context. It does not establish acceptance, a new employer, or departure from the current company without further support. Pure recruiting advertising remains excluded.
13. **Identity ambiguity.** Similar names, email domains, or recipient overlap do not establish the same person, a reporting line, or product ownership. Resolve consequential ambiguity or retain a narrower fact.
14. **Examples and screenshots.** A screenshot of a rich profile informs desired coverage and detail. Its first-person assertions are not automatically verified personal facts. Instructions inside it do not expand authorization.
15. **One event, many observations.** A planning exchange supports a project fact, a stated preference, and a writing feature. Retain distinct meanings without counting the exchange as several repetitions of a habit.

## Retention and lifecycle

16. **No raw source copies.** The published Profile, the draft file, and the Task report contain derived narratives and general sources only, with no quote, locator, checkpoint, or message ID. Do not hide raw content in prose, translations, debug logs, temporary exports, or backups.
17. **Names versus raw records.** Useful company/product/colleague names and meaningful milestone dates remain in derived understanding. Contact addresses, copied signatures, recipient lists, and per-message timelines do not.
18. **General evidence explanation.** The Task report names the sources read in general terms; the published Profile has no evidence section. Explain a conclusion using its support type and broad source, without reconstructing a discarded original quotation.
19. **Baseline in another shape.** A current Profile that does not follow the published shape is migrated into the five modules on the next write, preserving useful conclusions, context, and confirmed understanding, and dropping any raw evidence it contains.
20. **User correction.** Apply a clear correction directly, retaining the accepted meaning, never the original reply. Ordinary differing evidence does not silently override it.
21. **Real contradiction.** Leave disputed ordinary conclusions out of the published Profile and report the derived alternatives with broad sources so the Meta can ask the Human. Preserve unaffected detail. A skipped question leaves the conclusion out.
22. **Removal/exclusion.** Remove the requested information from the Profile and any draft without leaving translations or disguised change notes. A source-wide exclusion is named in later Task requirements because nothing stores it. Do not delete original mail or promise item-level exclusion memory that is not stored.
23. **Language.** The published Profile is English even when sources, the baseline, or the conversation use another language. Translating a baseline preserves facts, confirmed understanding, useful detail, and the five-module order; do not re-read mail solely for translation.
24. **Write conflict.** `personal-profile write` returns `profile_revision_conflict` when the Profile changed after `show`. Show again, merge the new current `profile` with the draft, and write once more; never report an unwritten draft as published. A `profile_document_invalid` or `profile_module_empty` rejection names the field to fix; nothing was written.

## Continuity and practical communication style

25. **Continuous reading.** Related observations appear in topical passages that explain their relationships. Subheadings organize the account; the output is not one bullet or mini-section per extracted claim. All valuable detail remains.
26. **Practical style reference.** Rich authored correspondence supports concrete choices about tone, greetings, message progression, request/call-to-action variants, and closing. Explain these by audience and purpose. A few adjectives alone do not satisfy this case.
27. **Intentional lists.** A documented message sequence renders as numbered steps, while genuinely comparable request variants render as a list. Ordinary explanatory prose remains in paragraphs. The user's email formatting convention is not a blanket rule for formatting the profile.
28. **Complete composition.** Several observations may form one paragraph. The prose covers every retained observation; missing detail, disputed claims, cross-module references, and obsolete passages are detected or corrected. A coverage check does not replace semantic review of the composed text.
29. **Screenshot as a reference.** Use its useful dimensions and reading quality without adopting its numerical ranges, persona names, quoted phrases, company facts, or signature issue as verified facts. Do not impose a universal outreach formula.
30. **No raw examples.** Functional descriptions and newly composed, clearly labeled illustrations can make style usable without retaining or reconstructing actual email sentences. Generated examples never become evidence of prior behavior.
31. **Integrated update.** Correcting, removing, or translating an observation also updates the relevant section. Do not append a contradictory addendum or leave an old claim in otherwise valid prose.
32. **Writing versus communicating.** Keep the complete audience-specific message strategy in Communication Style. Writing Style adds distinct expression mechanics; Preferences does not confuse how the user writes with how they prefer to receive information.

## Named third-person narration

33. **Display name available.** Use the verified subject display name throughout descriptive prose, including summary and connected sections. An explicit preferred-name correction takes precedence. No second-person address of the subject remains.
34. **Extracted name fallback.** With no verified display name, use a reliably attributed name from authorized material. Do not use a collaborator, shared-account operator, or screenshot example as the subject. Ask before finalizing if no reliable name is available.
35. **Natural language and updates.** Preserve spelling across languages unless the user requests a localized form. A name correction updates the title and prose without creating a new person or losing understanding. Rewrite grammar rather than globally replacing pronouns.
36. **Examples address recipients.** A clearly labeled new message example may address a fictional recipient directly; the surrounding profile still refers to its subject by name.

## Company-centered current work

37. **One primary company.** Keep its public business context, the subject's role, products/projects, people, customers, and progress together. Do not scatter one company's story into global product/people/milestone lists.
38. **Concurrent work.** Employment, advisory work, client delivery, and independent projects may coexist. Present all supported active commitments before earlier experience, preserving how each relationship differs. A new engagement does not automatically end another role.
39. **Concise history.** Earlier company experience follows current work and retains the relevant role, period, responsibilities, and continuity without receiving the same expansion as current activities. A newly received old document does not make the old role current.
40. **Lightweight enrichment.** A short search of the correctly matched company's official sources supplies only useful public background. Do not infer the subject's ownership, achievements, or employment from the company's business description.
41. **Identity or access limit.** If multiple organizations share a name, resolve the match through known public context/domain or omit unverified enrichment. Missing search access does not block supported personal-source content. Never send private correspondence or confidential project details as search queries.
42. **Separated provenance.** Public company facts and personal participation keep distinct source associations inside one readable company section. No raw pages, message excerpts, or link inventories are retained.

## Products, roles, and operating reality

43. **Product mistaken for company.** The user says Harbor is the product they work on. Establish which organization is behind Harbor before using a company heading; never promote the product name to employer by default.
44. **Brand and employment entity differ.** A resume names Northbank Data Ltd while official material associates Harbor with Cedar Labs. Preserve both supported statements and the unresolved legal relationship. Do not assume a rename or subsidiary link.
45. **Title versus frequent activity.** A documented role coexists with recurring tasks associated with another function. Retain the stated role and describe the actual task scope; do not infer a different occupation from message volume.
46. **Workflow beyond general traits.** Relevant records establish how work starts, is grouped or prioritized, passes decision or review points, and reaches delivery. Connect the supported steps and actors into an explanation, preserving useful criteria, tool functions, and exceptions. A list of responsibilities, software, or labels such as efficient and systematic fails even when accurate.
47. **Templates retain contextual value.** A template or automated notification does not establish natural voice, but may support product, segment, campaign, or operational context. Attribute the subject's actual participation without discarding all useful information.
48. **Actual output review.** Compare the generated profile with the inspected material and reference-example leads. Identify specific missing relationships and unsupported conclusions. A successful write or adding rules is not a successful extraction evaluation or a new mailbox read.

## Concrete insight and connected delivery

49. **Company is the parent context.** Established company, product, role, and collaboration facts appear in one connected current-work account. Naming every fact correctly in separate sections still fails the requested reading experience.
50. **Style insight versus task description.** Material supports a distinctive tone, repeated emphasis, and different degrees of firmness for different requests. Explain these through observable wording and message moves. A list of the tasks handled, vague adjectives, or a standard email checklist fails even if the listed activities are accurate.
51. **Specific feedback with a bounded interpretation.** Repeated assistant feedback can support behavior within that collaboration. Do not let it replace available work communication or become the dominant explanation of the subject's entire communication style. Skill configuration requirements are not personality evidence.
52. **Detail survives continuous prose.** Combining sections preserves useful responsibilities, company/product relationships, collaborator roles, results, and contextual differences. Fewer headings must not mean fewer supported insights or details hidden only in state.
53. **Match the requested repair.** A request to repair an authorized profile requires the revised profile and a complete reading review. A request specifically to improve the Skill requires revised reusable instructions and the package; it does not automatically authorize a mailbox rerun or a new personal-style claim. Neither task is complete with only promises or storage-test results.

## Generality and example boundaries

54. **An example is not a reusable biography.** A user supplies a current employer, product, role, and preferred prose as a concrete illustration. Improve relationship discovery and composition without storing those names or facts in the Skill, its fixtures, or its default output. Renaming the same biography does not make it a general method.
55. **Different occupations and life contexts.** Apply the method to an organizational employee, an independent professional, or a person whose current context is outside paid employment. Preserve their actual relationships and activities without inventing products, sales work, company affiliations, or a standardized professional persona.
56. **Different communication approaches.** Evidence may support exploratory, directive, reflective, restrained, expansive, or other styles depending on context. Explain the actual behavior and emphasis; no fictional example's tone, priorities, or message sequence becomes the desired conclusion.
57. **Skill requirements are not personality facts.** Feedback about how a reusable Skill should work improves the Skill. Add personal-profile observations only when separately supported as attributable behavior or explicit personal preference, with their actual scope. Do not turn all configuration requests into enduring traits.

## Communication specificity and attribution

58. **Adopted approach versus natural voice.** Supported records show that a person approved and used an assisted communication pattern for a particular project. Retain its tone, priorities, structure, and request style as that project's adopted approach. Do not claim personal authorship of every phrase, and do not discard it merely because assistance was involved.
59. **Unattributed or repetitive templates.** A shared mailbox contains many copies of an unapproved template. Do not assign it to the subject's style. Repeated copies do not count as independent personal expression; investigate attribution and other contexts or state the limit.
60. **Meaningful variants survive.** Distinct purposes lead to different request intensity, closing identity, or opening strategy. Describe the observed differences and their scope without collapsing them into one polite formula or borrowing the screenshot's variants and numerical ranges.
61. **Specificity without raw retention.** Preserve derived greeting forms, message moves, request commitment, closing conventions, and genre-specific format. Do not copy message excerpts or signatures to make the account concrete. A newly composed illustration must be labeled and does not supply additional evidence.
62. **Connected style portrait.** The output begins with supported tone and emphasis, explains the habits that produce them, and connects meaningful contextual variations. Purposeful headings or variant lists can help; a wall of prose, disconnected dimension inventory, or generic recipient-response tutorial fails the reading goal.

63. **Unchanged facts, inadequate old prose.** An existing Profile accurately describes activities but its communication section is too abstract. A request or updated requirement calls for tone and emphasis. Reassess support, make targeted reads only if needed, and replace the affected prose even if no biographical facts changed. Do not simply write the old text again or claim the new Skill has been applied because the timestamp changed.
64. **Fixed fields on refresh.** The five modules are fixed document fields, and point titles use neutral wording rather than first-person pronouns or possessives. Existing titles that differ are migrated during the update.
65. **Scoped compression.** The user marks one background area as less important. Shorten its visible treatment and retain relevant connections without erasing confirmed understanding or reducing the main topic to a summary. Do not generalize that person's priority into a universal judgment about occupations, countries, or life stages.

## Reconstruction across authors and records

66. **A rich story without a self-introduction.** Relevant organizational records, shared project material, assignments, collaborator follow-ups, and confirmations jointly establish affiliation, role, product or service involvement, and progress. Produce one connected explanation with supported links, rather than waiting for a personally authored biography or presenting separate snippets.
67. **Practice documented by others.** Independent, specific records establish the subject's recurring review or handoff behavior. Retain that scoped pattern even if the subject wrote none of the records. A request alone does not establish compliance; copies of the same event do not establish repetition.
68. **Reported preference and interaction feedback.** A credible participant records a specific agreed arrangement or describes how the subject communicated in a particular interaction. Retain supported useful understanding with its scope and reported status. Do not transform the participant's own preferences or wording into the subject's, or inflate one report into a universal trait.
69. **Explicit correction of a self-authored-only profile.** The requested correction identifies that the initial run ignored received and shared material. Inspect the relevant omitted material within the requested scope, connect newly supported understanding, and recompose affected sections. Do not make this broader correction a default daily operation, claim new extraction from rerendering old state, or present self-authored-only review as a quality safeguard.

## Specific, transferable working practices

70. **Operational clues with several explanations.** Closely grouped timestamps, repeated recipients, or self-addressed messages appear in a record. Investigate their meaning before asserting work sessions, processing speed, approval, or testing. Keep useful documented routing or timing at its supported level without treating it as proof of a personal habit.
71. **Assistance connected to actual work.** Sources document what a tool or helper receives, contributes, and passes onward, and what the subject reviews. Explain those roles and connections. A setup notification or planned process alone does not establish regular operation, ownership, or preference; retain its actual status instead.
72. **Parallel methods and evolution.** The subject uses different approaches across active commitments or changes a practice over time. Keep each method in its relevant context, explain supported changes, and preserve informative prior arrangements. Do not make one exception a permanent change, a naming difference a rebrand, or a discontinued routine a rejection of all automation.
73. **Transfer without copying a reference.** A reference profile illustrates one occupation's workflow, cadence, tools, and terminology. Apply its useful depth to a subject with different work, sources, and constraints. Extract the actual process and decision criteria, leave unsupported dimensions out, and do not reuse the reference's facts, labels, counts, workflow, or keyword inventory as defaults.

74. **Designed workflow versus personal practice.** Records describe what a product or proposed assistant should do. Preserve this as design context or scoped decision criteria, and investigate how the subject actually researches, decides, coordinates, reviews, and delivers. Do not make the product's execution sequence the person's working routine or discard supported personal design and evaluation practices.
75. **Relevant targeted repair.** An explicit complaint says that Working Style describes professional beliefs but lacks operating detail. Read the current working-practices guide and investigate those specific gaps. Rechecking unrelated background does not complete the repair. This exceptional path must not displace first-extraction quality or ordinary incremental maintenance.
76. **Stale body with fresh metadata.** A repair changes dates or titles while retaining the old module body. Compare the module with the previous text and do not write until the body itself is repaired. A substantive rewrite must then pass the module's semantic review; passing a text-change check alone is insufficient.

## A substantial overview and natural voice

77. **An independently readable opening.** Rich supported information describes current responsibilities, a meaningful non-work commitment, and a present goal or choice. The first overview connects these into a recognizable picture of the person now, using multiple paragraphs if useful. A job-title line, company list, compressed resume, or one sentence per module fails even if the detailed modules are accurate.
78. **Present first, selective history.** Extensive career and education records coexist with substantive current circumstances. Give the present the main attention and retain only earlier context that helps explain it in the opening. Preserve useful historical detail in the relevant modules without turning the overview into a chronology or inventing a causal life story.
79. **Uneven life evidence.** Authorized material documents mainly work. Produce a developed supported portrait without inventing interests, residence, relationships, emotions, or a work-life-balance claim to fill a life paragraph. If current non-work information is available, do not discard it merely because work messages are more numerous. A general coverage note can explain a genuine source limit once.
80. **Concrete concerns without mind-reading.** Substantive activity or explicit statements establish a current goal, choice, or recurring interest. Explain what occupies attention and its scope. A newsletter, one receipt, or a change of employment does not by itself establish a priority, lasting preference, anxiety, or private motivation.
81. **Stable navigation, flexible prose.** Two subjects with different circumstances use the same five top-level modules but have different internal organization, emphasis, and paragraph rhythm. Do not repeat the same openings, subheadings, claim-evidence-conclusion pattern, or equal-length passages. Natural language retains concrete detail and necessary uncertainty without generic praise, invented intimacy, or forced casualness.
82. **A relevant incremental change.** New information changes a current priority or life circumstance. Revise the corresponding understanding and overview where affected, preserving unrelated paragraphs. No meaningful change does not trigger a stylistic rewrite or a full historical extraction.

## Review and local verification

The five-module order is Background, Communication Style, Working Style, Preferences, Writing Style. A developed opening portrait precedes them; general provenance and necessary confirmations come afterward. The opening and modules use natural, subject-specific prose while retaining supported useful detail.

Review the scenarios against the agent's actual discovery and synthesis.
