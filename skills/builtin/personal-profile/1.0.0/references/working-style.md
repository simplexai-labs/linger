# Reconstruct concrete working practices

Use this reference when extracting, composing, or repairing Working Style. Explain how the subject gets work done in a recognizable context, including the sequence, practical choices, participants, and changes that matter. A list of responsibilities, software names, or desirable traits does not explain a working method.

Apply the method to the subject's actual activities: individual practice, company work, service delivery, research, study, creative work, care, volunteering, or other commitments. Batch outreach, formal approval, automation, and corporate hierarchies are possible findings, never defaults. The dimensions below are discovery routes, not a mandatory questionnaire, set of headings, or quota of claims.

## Build a concrete account on the first extraction

For a new subject, read this guide before source selection and discover actual working practices directly from the authorized material. Do not require an existing section to criticize before investigating rhythm, decisions, collaboration, assistance, and follow-through. A first profile containing only professional beliefs, product features, or career history is incomplete when useful operating evidence is available.

During initial discovery, track which relevant aspects are supported, unexamined, or inaccessible and use promising gaps to guide reading. Prioritize substantive activity records and complete exchanges; easy identity or administrative confirmations do not replace them unless their content explains the relevant practice. Deliver the supported operating detail in the first profile rather than waiting for daily updates to make the account useful.

On later incremental runs, compare new information with the established account. Add a newly documented handoff, changed routine, or meaningful exception where it belongs; preserve unaffected methods and prose. A new concrete practice is not a duplicate of a broad statement about collaboration or quality. Reopen older source context only when needed to interpret the change. Do not repeat all initial discovery because the section exists or its guidance has been updated. An explicit complaint is handled through the separate targeted correction path.

## Reconstruct the process across sources

Start with the main current responsibility or recurring activity. Trace its supported connections from inputs and constraints through choices, action, collaboration, and follow-through. Use relevant received and sent messages, shared documents, task or calendar records, feedback, and system notices regardless of author. Combine complementary pieces even when no single source describes the whole process.

Distinguish a proposed process, an assigned procedure, an observed instance, and a recurring practice. Evidence of the subject's participation matters; personally authored descriptions are not required. Keep unsupported steps open instead of inventing a neat end-to-end workflow. Preserve useful observations that cover only part of a process.

Also distinguish **the subject's way of working** from **a process the subject designs or describes for something else**. A product that asks clarifying questions, a proposed automated pipeline, or instructions for a team do not prove that the subject personally works in that sequence. The subject's actual research, prioritization, design decisions, testing, and review practices can still be valid Working Style observations when evidenced. Retain product-design intent as professional context or clearly scoped decision criteria; do not relabel product behavior as personal operating behavior.

| Dimension | What to discover when relevant | Detail worth retaining |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting and framing | What initiates work and what the subject needs before acting | Requests, observations, briefs, constraints, clarification, and definition of the next useful result |
| Rhythm and grouping | How work is scheduled, grouped, sequenced, or interrupted | Recurring cycles, batch versus individual handling, priority changes, parallel work, and supported differences by task |
| Decisions and standards | What determines an action, choice, or readiness to proceed | Validation steps, comparison criteria, tradeoffs, review thresholds, and circumstances that change the decision |
| Collaboration and handoffs | Who prepares, decides, reviews, implements, or receives work | Actual responsibility boundaries, checkpoints, dependencies, handoff contents, and how questions or corrections return |
| Tools and assistance | What a tool, system, assistant, or collaborator contributes at a particular step | Its input, function, output, connection to the next step, and what the subject checks or controls |
| Delivery and follow-through | How work reaches the next person or intended outcome | Readiness checks, delivery, acknowledgment, follow-up, revision, and documented completion |
| Context and parallel commitments | Where the method changes across active activities | Company, project, audience, urgency, or resource differences, without blending separate workflows into a universal habit |
| Evolution | How an established practice changes over time | Supported adoption, adjustment, delegation, replacement, or discontinuation; distinguish current practice from history and plans |

Look for decisions that make the process distinctive. Where supported, connect the action to its practical function, such as making review possible before a handoff. Do not invent psychological motives, claim an intended benefit was achieved, or turn a constraint imposed by a team into the person's preference.

## Interpret operational clues at their supported level

- **Rhythm and scale:** retain a supported cadence or useful approximate volume when it explains the method. Compare independent sequences within comparable activities and periods before describing a routine or a change. Grouped timestamps may reflect scheduling, imports, automated runs, or duplicate records; they do not alone prove a personal work session, speed, or time commitment. State the observed scope and avoid unsupported numerical precision or universal frequency. For mailbox-specific response, follow-through, and attention patterns, use [Temporal patterns in context](gmail-single-source.md#observe-temporal-patterns-in-context).
- **Routing and review:** recipients, copied addresses, forwarding, and self-addressed messages can suggest where to investigate. Establish an actual review, approval, delegation, test, or delivery step from content or corroborating records before naming it as such. Describe participant roles rather than retaining contact addresses.
- **Tools and automation:** connect tools to the work they support. A notification, account, installed integration, or synchronization result can establish a specific system event without proving that the subject designed, operated, preferred, or regularly used the system. Separate a planned workflow from one put into use, and record human involvement only where supported.
- **Change and continuity:** describe the present practice first, followed by earlier arrangements when the contrast explains it. A one-off exception is not automatically a lasting change. Disabling one routine does not imply abandoning automation; silence or lack of recent records does not establish discontinuation. Preserve useful history without presenting it as current.
- **Recurring topics and terminology:** retain a recurring concern when it explains decisions, scope, or method. Vocabulary in templates, marketing material, or other people's messages does not by itself establish the subject's expertise or priorities. A long keyword list is not a work pattern. Distinctive language belongs in Writing Style when attributable; domain context belongs in Background when it does not explain a working practice.
- **Changing names or labels:** resolve company, product, project, and tool identity before interpreting a changed label as a rebrand or strategic shift. Naming history normally belongs with the relevant Background account. Include it in Working Style only when it establishes a supported change in how work is performed.

Reference profiles and screenshots can reveal useful dimensions or a helpful level of specificity. Do not copy their personal facts, addresses, dates, counts, tool stacks, categories, speculative causal explanations, or event sequences into the reusable Skill or a new subject's profile. Learn the questions to investigate, not the answers.

## Compose a connected explanation

Lead with the main supported way the subject approaches the relevant work. Explain how the work begins and proceeds, where choices or collaboration shape it, and how it is reviewed, delivered, or adjusted where known. Keep concrete detail at the point where it explains the process. Do not force a complete sequence when the evidence supports only particular stages.

Use natural topical subsections when the evidence warrants them, such as work rhythm, collaboration, tool use, or changing practices. Choose headings for the subject's actual work; do not reproduce the dimension table as the finished profile. Short lists can clarify a real sequence or compare variants, but connect them through explanatory prose. Do not produce a message-by-message timeline.

When different current commitments have materially different methods, anchor each to the company or activity already established in Background. Explain shared practices once and retain meaningful differences. Give the main current work appropriate depth, cover other active work, then include only earlier methods that help explain the present. Do not let the most message-heavy activity crowd out a less documented but important parallel commitment.

Background explains where the person works, on what, with whom, and with which responsibility. Working Style explains how those responsibilities are carried out. Communication Style explains interpersonal tone and message strategy. Preferences explains supported likes, choices, and desired arrangements. Use these distinctions to place details coherently rather than repeating full accounts across modules.

### Independently synthetic example

The following museum setting is fictional and illustrates specificity, not a default process or persona.

Weak: Avery is organized, collaborative, and detail-oriented.

More specific: For exhibition installations, Avery first checks the venue's access windows and outstanding measurements, then groups tasks by the spaces available. Unresolved dimensions go back to the design team before fabrication, while confirmed items move to the workshop. Avery checks the installation list before the on-site handoff and incorporates fit corrections into the next version. Short-notice repairs are handled individually, so the grouped planning approach applies to scheduled installations rather than every task.

The passage connects a starting condition, grouping choice, decision checkpoint, handoff, feedback, and exception. It does not need tool names, exact counts, or a claim about personality to be concrete. A real account must support every corresponding detail; this example must not be transplanted by replacing names.

## Check the actual output

- Can a new reader explain how the subject carries out at least the supported parts of an important activity, including the meaningful decisions or handoffs?
- Does each claimed practice contain a recognizable action and context, rather than only a trait label or a list of tasks and tools?
- Were useful details about rhythm, standards, collaboration, assistance, delivery, and change retained wherever the sources support them, without forcing unsupported dimensions?
- Are the subject's actions distinguished from team rules, requests, system events, proposals, and completed outcomes?
- Are recurring patterns, one-off actions, current methods, prior arrangements, and parallel activities accurately scoped?
- Does the prose form an explanation, with useful detail visible to the reader and only general evidence-source summaries?

Apply these checks before the first delivery and to passages affected by new information during incremental updates. If a first draft remains abstract despite available evidence, deepen its discovery and synthesis before delivery. An explicit complaint about an existing section follows the separate targeted correction path; it does not make whole-section rewriting a daily requirement.
